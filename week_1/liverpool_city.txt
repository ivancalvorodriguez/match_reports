Liverpool F.C. vs Manchester City F.C.: How did City play through Liverpool’s press, and who won the duel?
Premier League · 11/10/2026 · 0-3

With new managers at both clubs, I wanted to see how Liverpool press and how City planned to play through it. Previously, under Guardiola, possession was key, and I wanted to see whether that was still the case; in the Klopp era, pressing was the team’s hallmark.

My analysis covers only the period before Liverpool’s red card, because the first and second goals came very soon afterwards, and the pressing that followed was driven more by pride than by tactics.

From the first goal kick, in the fourth minute, Manchester City’s structure was clear. They formed a diamond: the goalkeeper, the two centre-backs and a defensive midfielder, with the full-backs wide in case they needed to provide width.

Liverpool’s press was set up to match that structure. Their striker stayed between the two centre-backs, blocking the passing lane between them, and when the goalkeeper had the ball, he used his position to cut off the pass to one of the centre-backs. The wingers went with City’s full-backs, and one of Liverpool’s central midfielders followed City’s defensive midfielder.

City’s idea was to find the defensive midfielder and switch play to the side away from the ball, neutralising the opposition’s press.

This happened again in the 18th minute, although this time they went long to bypass Liverpool’s press. They tried to play short passes for as long as they could, but if they needed to play a long ball up to the striker or out wide, they would do so.

In conclusion, I think City won the duel: Liverpool may have been the better side in that spell, but City’s build-up was successful, and at least in that phase they had no problems against the press. City built up with a back four, the full-backs wide and level with the defensive midfielder, who offered support to help beat the press; this was the key to their successful build-up play. They always wanted to stretch the pitch so that the defensive midfielder felt more comfortable when he received the ball. They preferred collective play to individual play, even though they have players like Cherki who could make the difference on their own.

To check whether I am right, you can look at the average positions of the players I have discussed (the centre-backs, the full-backs and the defensive midfielder; I also discussed the goalkeeper, but you cannot draw any conclusions about him from this). Bear in mind that they cover the whole match, including the period after the red card, so they are only a rough guide to City’s build-up before it.
