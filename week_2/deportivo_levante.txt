RC Deportivo de A Coruña vs Levante UD: How did Deportivo try to progress the ball against Levante’s block, and through which channel did it work best?
LaLiga · 16/10/2026 · 1-1

Before kick-off, I wanted to answer this question because, in previous games, Deportivo had built up with short passes and I wanted to see how they did it.

At the beginning of the match, Deportivo’s structure was clear. They built up with a back three (the two centre-backs and the right full-back, Ximo Navarro) and two defensive midfielders in front of it to provide support.

They wanted to play through the midfielders and break the first line of pressure. If they could not, they were happy to go long. Their main idea, though, was to play through the defensive midfielders, find the forwards quickly and attack the opposition goal.

That was their initial plan, but around the 20-minute mark Deportivo switched to a completely different structure. They built up with a back four: the two centre-backs plus the full-backs, who stayed wide and deep. This was the change that paid off for Antonio Hidalgo, Deportivo’s head coach. They stuck with it until the final whistle, and it forced Levante to decide whether one of their wingers would press the centre-back or the full-back.

Levante’s press was close to man-to-man. Against the first structure, they put the striker on the centre-back, the wingers on the other centre-backs and two midfielders on the defensive midfielders. Against the second, one of the wingers stepped up alongside the striker and, on the free flank, Levante’s full-back pushed onto Deportivo’s full-back. In both pressing schemes, they did not press constantly; the first pass was their trigger.

The second structure was key to Deportivo’s goal. The move began with the left centre-back, Bright Ede, driving forward with the ball. In this structure he was the free centre-back, so he had time to carry it and escape Levante’s press. From there, he played the ball into the forwards, in this case Yeremay, who received it in the central channel.

This example also answers the question of which channel worked best for Deportivo. In my view, it was the central channel: in the second structure they had four players there, and passes into that channel were easier to play. To check whether my conclusion holds up, you can look at the passes the centre-backs made.

- Iván Calvo Rodríguez
